<H3></H3>

The most meaningful lesson I’ve learned about leadership didn’t happen in an office; it happened over a chessboard Casa de Amistad . Casa de Amistad is a non-profit organization that offers academic support and guidance for students facing educational and economic challenges within San Diego. I was mentoring a young boy who really struggled with paying attention and was falling behind in school. I decided to teach him how to play chess, and I was amazed at how much it captured his focus. During a Christmas gift handout later that year, out of all the toys available, he chose a chess set. He was so excited that he ran around telling everyone he was going to go home and teach his younger brother how to play. Now, every time I come to Casa to tutor him, he asks to spend the last ten minutes playing chess. Seeing that change in him showed me how much of an impact one person can have on another. However, it made me realize that while I love helping individuals, I also want to work on a larger scale to help entire communities through politics or city planning

<H3></H3>

This desire to make a bigger impact is what led me to intern for APAC and Supervisor Monica Montgomery Steppe last summer. The Summer Youth Academy with Supervisor Montgomery Steppe was an incredible experience where I met interesting people and saw how San Diego actually functions. During the two-week program, we focused heavily on the issue of homelessness. After listening to presentations from local non-profits such as Father Joe's Villages, I worked with my fellow interns to develop a potential solution to homelessness in San Diego. We drafted a mock board letter and created a formal presentation, which we then presented to the District 4 staff. Getting to share our ideas with the people who actually run the city was a huge moment for me. Combined with attending a homeless resource fair in person, this experience changed the way I think about my community and showed me how I can use my leadership to solve big problems for thousands of people at once.

<H3></H3>

My commitment to San Diego actually started long before that internship or Casa. One of my first experiences was with Feeding San Diego, packaging food for families. Even though I was young, I understood that a few hours of my time could change someone’s life, and I fell in love with volunteering. In middle school, I joined TVIA, and for the past four years, I’ve been doing everything from neighborhood cleanups to food distribution. These experiences taught me that simply being present and helping out can make a lasting difference.
